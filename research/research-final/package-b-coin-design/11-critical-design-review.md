### 1.5 Desk-test verdict

The rules can be played card by card without a referee, after five clarifications. The draft as written produced a degenerate result: a State threshold score in the first Propaganda round from sensible play. The Troops requirement (kept for CT 6.3.1 fidelity) does not remove it: a State that plans for the rule still scores 42 in round 1. With Resources left and three State-friendly C2 cards (DK-024, DK-026 and DK-032 each give Legitimacy +2), the State will very likely pass 42 at the round 2 check unless the PKK Coerces Support spaces. That fails the 10, section 8, test 2 criterion on paper. Confidence: medium. One run, one player.

ARGK variant: after DK-001 the PKK had 8 domestic Guerrillas and the Botan Base. Under the first fix ("in domestic spaces") ARGK was still legal on card 2. It beat the logged play: +3 Resources, +4 Guerrillas, the State loses the DK-012 Event and the PKK keeps First Eligible. The precondition now also needs 2 domestic PKK Bases. In this log that point comes on card 8.

### 1.6 Ambiguities and degenerate points found

1. ARGK Formed needed "8 or more Guerrillas on the map". The setup counts external boxes as on the map (10, section 2.2 totals), so the Pivotal was legal on card 1 in Aug 1984 for an event of Oct 1986 [E-0075]. With "in domestic spaces" alone it was still legal and dominant on card 2. Fixed: 8 Guerrillas in domestic spaces and 2 domestic PKK Bases. Timing stays a watch item.
2. Air Mobility "among up to 3 domestic spaces" did not say whether origins count. Fixed to the CT 4.2.2 reading.
3. Western Cities lockout. The PKK cannot March there, may Rally in a City only at Neutral or Opposition, and Western Cities starts at Passive Support. No card shifts it or places PKK pieces there (deck search). So 3 to 6 State points were safe for the whole game. Fixed: Rally in Western Cities is legal at Passive Support [tune].
4. Responder matrix. The Event row was labeled "design choice". The CT playbook twelfth-card example shows Op + SA or Pass after a Pivotal in the Event box. Relabeled. Note 1 described a British Way option (select the Event box, then pass) that this track cannot do. Rewritten.
5. Martial Law and OHAL drain -1, and the restraint gain gives +1 whenever no Displacement marker flips. In C1 and C2 the Emergency Rule costs nothing. Open (proposal in section 3).
6. State Resources run dry by card 4, then the State earns 19 against 6 per round. Ops cost the State 2 per space and the PKK 1. Open watch item.
7. Train option 3 turns 2 Police into a State Base for 2 Resources. In Şanlıurfa this adds Pop 4 to State income at every round. Open watch item.
8. Attack with 6 or more Guerrillas never fails (die result at most 6). CT has the same rule. Logged only.
9. Campaign length. With the Propaganda card at position 9 to 13, a Campaign has 8 to 16 cards (12 + this pile's position - last pile's position). DD-13.1 says about 9 to 12. Proposed DD text change.

### 1.7 Full-campaign arithmetic

1. Piece counts in setups A, B and C match the inventory and the printed totals. Control and scores in all three tables recompute correctly.
2. Pop values recompute from 1990 totals with half-up rounding and a minimum of 1: sum 29 plus 3 for Western Cities.
3. Thresholds recomputed by the one-third rule at review time (42 / 28, 41 / 34 and 38 / 40). After this review the coordinator adopted the easy-ceiling rule and a Civic Action cap of Legitimacy / 2 (DD-18): 49 / 30, 48 / 41 and 44 / 48.
4. Resources over five Campaigns, no events: State about 19 a round at start, more with each new Base in a Plain or Mountain space. PKK about 6 at start, then lower: the KDP order and the Syria protocol cut host income, and Capture removes 1 more. Estimate: State 95 to 130, PKK 20 to 30 plus Extort. The cost gap is 2 to 1, the income gap is 3 or 4 to 1. Low confidence; design currency only.
5. Legitimacy under OHAL Extended Powers: -2 drain, +1 restraint, net -1 per round before any Clearance. A State that never clears loses at most 3 points over C3 to C5 from the drain.

## 2. Findings by perspective

### 2.1 Historian of Turkey

1. Start conditions are defensible: martial law in force since Dec 1978 and Law 2935 from 1983 [E-0039; E-0052]. The OHAL card date is right [E-0055].
2. Batman and Şırnak names are handled as regions, with a scenario note (03, section 7). Good practice.
3. The fixed C2 cards give three Legitimacy +2 gains (local elections, the lawyer access decree, refugees admitted). Each is sourced. Together they make 1987 to 1989 a good period for State Legitimacy. That reading is arguable for a period with documented custody abuse [E-0295]. Watch in play.

### 2.2 Kurdish studies

1. Support and Opposition are labeled as abstractions of active minorities (DD-06.2). Good.
2. Offices block all PKK Agitation in their space but only cap State Support at Passive. The legal party thus harms the PKK more than the State. This is a quiet claim about C-025 (party autonomy) through a mechanic. Proposed DD-10.5 change: an Office caps its space at Passive Support and at Passive Opposition, and blocks Agitation only beyond Passive Opposition.
3. Guards are State pieces with no own aim [F-057]. The deck adds guard refusal without violence (DK-031). Good.

### 2.3 Security studies

1. Detection before destruction, the Mountain modifier and Guards that help detection but not Assault fit the evidence base [F-049; F-059].
2. Cross-Border places a Disputed marker that records "unknown". That is honest.
3. Village Clearance gives activation and a Rally block. Its threshold denial value is not costed (section 3).

### 2.4 Quantitative

1. All arithmetic in setups, thresholds and Pop checks out (section 1.7).
2. The threshold method assumes both sides grow at a similar rate. The desk test shows the State grows much faster, because its Support sits in safe Plain spaces and Western Cities. The one-third rule gives equal gaps, not equal difficulty.
3. Five-level Support doubles the headroom of every space against CT. Test 2 in 04, section 6 (count Active once) should run early.

### 2.5 COIN designer

1. The CT initiative track is applied correctly. Pass, Limited Op and Event keep the First Eligible faction First (CT 2.3.5).
2. Civic Action was looser than CT 6.3.1. Fixed for the Propaganda phase, but the fix does not change the pace problem.
3. The Base income term rewards Train option 3 too well. Try "Pop of spaces with State Control, a State Base and Troops".
4. Stale cross-references in 03, 05, 08 and the deck notes were fixed.

### 2.6 Playtester

1. The Legitimacy phase has eight steps. In C1 and C2 most of them do nothing. A C1 and C2 play aid can skip steps 2 to 5.
2. The State is idle for two cards per Campaign for lack of Resources. That gives the PKK free tempo. It may be fine. Log Pass counts (05, section 10, test 3).
3. The PKK path to score is clear: March into a lightly held space, Rally a Base, Agitate in the Rally. The State path is clear: Train with Civic Action in Pop 4 spaces. Both are understandable in one game.

### 2.7 Ethics

1. No action adds score for removing pieces or for harm. The relative test in 09, section 7.1 holds.
2. Village Clearance can still decide a game by denial at a threshold check. That is a reward for emptying a space in outcome terms, if not in points. Proposed DD-07.2 change in section 3.
3. Harm actions can flip Control and so open or close Civic Action and Agitation. 09, section 7.2 now names this. The PKK Harm marker self-block covers Strike Collaborators in its own space only.
4. Block-only sides for the harmer on T2 cards are a good rule. DK-018 (contested Pınarcık killings) gives its PKK side a Redress anywhere for 2 Resources. That is the normal Agitation price, so no discount. Logged.

### 2.8 Traceability

1. Thirty factual sentences across 20 Package A files were checked against their rows: 24 match, 3 minor mismatches, 3 overstated. Four one-line corrections were applied. Details are in the audit file.
2. E-0082 has confidence Moderate in its field but its notes say Low until a primary source is seen. Proposed data fix.
3. Every cited ID in the chapters exists (validator: 0 unknown IDs).

### 3.2 Risks from the input reviews that still apply

1. **Weak evidence base for effects.** Every card effect and every number is a hypothesis (DD-15.1). Card events are sourced; card sizes are not. Risk high for values, moderate for facts.
2. **Historical determinism.** Seven mandatory structural cards fix the large changes in order. This is honest for law and institutions (Law 3175, OHAL, HEP). It is less safe for hosts. The Syria protocol card always moves Syria to Tolerant, but its shaded side can move it back. Capture fires only if the Leadership is Abroad, so it is conditional, as the data call the sanctuary-capture link an inference [F-021]. Do not tune thresholds so that the historical path wins.
3. **The opposite error.** A design where the PKK wins easily would imply that a small change was enough. The data do not show that. The desk test shows the reverse risk now: the State wins too easily.
4. **False balance.** The inventory is about 2 to 1, and real ratios were larger (04, section 1). Both armed sides carry harm costs, and the Harm limit is 6 for each. DD-15.3 says equal costs do not claim equal acts. Card text must name each act with its evidence level.
5. **False precision.** Tracks and thresholds suggest measurement. No source number becomes a threshold (M-028). Keep the "[design]" label on every Pop value.
6. **Normalizing violence.** Village Clearance, Covert Action and Strike Collaborators carry plain print names and costs. Players may still treat them as tools. A content note and an opt-out rule for T2 cards are needed before any public playtest (08, section 7.2).
7. **Exoticism.** Kurds are not one block: the PKK, Guards, the legal parties, Hizbullah and tribes had opposed interests [F-057]. The two-player core keeps legal politics as a non-player system. That loses a human advocate for legal actors (Sol review). MOD-3B is the tested remedy.
8. **Source bias.** Most data are in English. Turkish and Kurdish-language sources are under-represented. Several C1 cards rest on one source family (08, section 10).
9. **Consultation.** No Kurdish or Turkish scholars, civil-society members or survivors have reviewed the design. This must happen before any public release.

## 4. Counterfactual play paths the rules allow

The deck fixes the frame. Players change the board, not the facts on a card. A counterfactual result is a game result, not evidence about the past. Plausible for game purposes means: the actor existed, held the instrument and faced the choice. It does not mean likely.

| Path | Rules that allow it | What the data show | What the data do not show |
|---|---|---|---|
| State never uses Village Clearance | Clearance is an optional SA; restraint gain +1 per round | Clearance cut PKK logistics early and cost later [F-050] | What a different field policy would have done |
| Earlier Normalizing | After the OHAL card the State may step to Normalizing; drain 0, Return legal | The emergency region shrank from 1994 [F-014] | Whether earlier normalization would have reduced violence |
| Ceasefire holds into Talks | Declare Ceasefire from C3; Reciprocal Restraint; Talks; Negotiated Outcome (07, section 7) | The 1993 ceasefire was declared and collapsed [E-0089; F-038]; the State recognized none [F-039] | Whether any ceasefire could have held; responsibility for the collapse is disputed |
| Legal party never banned | Closure filing is a State choice (07, section 6.3) | Each closure was followed by a successor [F-052] | Whether the legal channel would have reduced the war |
| PKK avoids coercion | Coerce and Strike Collaborators are optional | Both armed sides pressed the same villages [F-034] | The political payoff of restraint |
| Syria pressure earlier or later | Diplomacy SA from C1 (with -1 before C5); Pivotal from C5 | Syria yielded in 1998 under threats [F-020] | Whether earlier pressure would have worked |
| KDP and PUK kept apart | Separate posture tracks; no automatic link (DD-09.1) | The hosts acted on their own interests [F-051] | Exact timing of any alternative alignment |
| Leadership never Abroad | Capture needs Abroad; Present makes the Always line do nothing | Capture followed the departure from Syria [F-021] | That capture was inevitable or impossible |

Excluded by scope: a Turkish invasion of Syria, a pan-Kurdish state and an all-Kurdish coalition player. These are scope choices, not claims about what actors imagined.

Debrief questions after each game:

1. Which card most changed your plan?
2. Which action cost you later, and did the delay surprise you?
3. What could the actors do that the game did not let you do?
4. What did the game let you do that the actors could not?
5. Which result do you think the game got wrong?

Testing rule: record every win that used an unhistorical path. A high win rate for one path means the rules reward a path the history does not support or fail to model its cost. Treat both as design bugs, never as a finding about history.

## 5. Residual risks

1. **Critical: State threshold pace.** Even with the Troops rule, the State reaches its threshold in round 1 on paper, so a State win at the first victory check (round 2) is likely. Thresholds are [tune] under DD-14.6. Two options to test first: (a) Propaganda Civic Action cap = Legitimacy / 2, round down; (b) State threshold computed from a "contested maximum" that leaves out Western Cities. Run 10, section 8, test 2 before any other tuning.
2. **Major: income asymmetry and the Base term.** Log income per Campaign. If the State ends any Campaign with more than 20 unspent Resources in more than one game in three, cut the base income from 8 to 6.
3. **Major: threshold denial by Village Clearance.** Proposed DD-07.2 change. Until then, log every Clearance made when the PKK is within 2 x Pop of its threshold.
4. **Moderate: Office asymmetry** (proposed DD-10.5 change).
5. **Moderate: Emergency Rule has no cost in C1 and C2.** Test the restraint-gain change.
6. **Moderate: C1 has little draw variety** (12 of 15 cards). DK-013 and DK-014 were verified in R4 and DK-100 was added, which closed the DD-13.2 shortfall.
7. **Moderate: Western Cities fix is untested.** The new Rally right may make Western Cities a cheap PKK target. Count Rally and Coerce there (03, section 8, test 3).
8. **Minor: CT ambiguity on responder locations** (05, section 2.3, note 2) is now labeled a design choice.
9. **Not tested here:** C3 to C5 systems (Legal Politics, ceasefire, Covert Action, Capture). The next desk test should start Scenario B and play one full Legal Politics phase and one ceasefire step.

Next step: run Scenario A twice with sides swapped and the fixes in place. Log the round 2 margins (10, section 8, test 1).

### 6.8 Verdict

1. Playability: the C3 and C4 rules can be played card by card. Fourteen text defects needed a ruling or a fix. Most sat in 07 (Legal Politics, ceasefire, Emergency Rule).
2. Balance (paper only): Scenario B tilts hard to the PKK. The State peaked at 26 on card 2 and fell to 10 by the end of the C3 round. Drivers: Coerce (card 8), Legitimacy drains with Europe fixed at 5 and a Propaganda Civic Action cap of 0 at Legitimacy 0 or 1. About 6 points of the Legitimacy loss came from harm tools used to exercise them (Clearance and the Covert reveal). A State that skips both and is not hit by a PKK filing ends C3 near Legitimacy 8 and a score of about 18 to 24 (estimate), still far from 48. The PKK at 26 needs 15 more and lost 5 net over the first 5 C4 cards against heavy State spending. Expect margins to decide the game, with a clear PKK result. Confidence: low (one run, one player).
3. Ethics: no direct score for harm. Three indirect payoffs: a ban forced by the PKK (fixed), free State harm at Legitimacy 0 (fixed) and Extort income after a Strike Collaborators Control flip (logged, IR-13 pattern). Village Clearance never paid.
4. Legal Politics (DD-02 test): not passive on cards, because both armed players raced to set levels before Offices arrived. In the Propaganda phase only the State has a lever and no reason to use it. This is one data point toward the DD-02 flip trigger, not a trigger.
