# Colonial Twilight: Lessons for the Kurdish Insurgency Design

**Date:** 25 October 2026
**Scope:** The consolidated 1984–1999 research and proposed 1993–1995 prototype.
**Status:** Critical comparison and proposed revisions. No playtesting or balance is claimed.

## Executive assessment

**Use Colonial Twilight as our primary mechanical reference, but not as a template for the conflict's history or political outcome.** It strengthens the two-player State–PKK recommendation. Its greatest value is how it makes military capacity, political effects and initiative compete.

There is also a significant qualification: the earlier comparison missed *The Guerrilla Generation*, published in 2026. Its Peru game addresses emergency institutions, local self-defence forces and leadership vulnerability. It is a serious competing reference. Its final rulebook was inaccessible during this review, so its detailed mechanisms remain unverified. See [GMT's publisher description](https://www.gmtgames.com/p-1032-the-guerrilla-generation.aspx).

Below, **historical findings** refer to the [consolidated claim registry](research/research-final/sources/claim-registry.csv); **proposals** are untested design judgments. Mechanical citations refer to the official [May 2018 rules](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/gmtwebsiteassets/colonialtwilight/ColTwi-RULES-LR-May2018.pdf) and [October 2018 playbook](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/gmtwebsiteassets/colonialtwilight/ColTwi-PLAYBOOK-Oct2018.pdf).

Confidence is High for the checked rule descriptions, Moderate for the adaptation judgments and Low for a definitive ranking of every published COIN game.

## Where the historical analogy works

Both designs involve a militarily stronger state confronting an insurgent organization whose survival depends partly on concealment, political organization and external access. Mountain regions, cities, local auxiliaries and civilian coercion create useful comparative questions.

The most useful shared distinction is **armed control versus political acceptance**. *Colonial Twilight* already separates these. Its designer also explains that Support and Opposition abstract mixed attitudes and active minorities, rather than unanimous regional preferences. Our design should preserve that caution and distinguish legal participation and civilian safety as well.
*Rules 1.6–1.7; playbook pp.13–14; historical finding C0012.*

The institutional comparison is particularly valuable. Brian Train considered additional factions representing the French Army, settler interests and rival nationalism. He instead represented their effects through events and systems because he judged their sustained agency insufficiently distinct for separate players.
*Playbook p.12, “Structure: a two-player COIN game.”*

That supports our **method for selecting factions**. It does not prove that French and Turkish institutions were equivalent.

## Where the analogy breaks

| Issue | Why direct transfer would mislead |
|---|---|
| **Political endpoint** | CT's colonial setting shapes Commitment, metropolitan deployment and its interpretation of final bargaining. Our game must accommodate territorial integrity, changing PKK objectives, political rights and continued conflict without assuming a decolonization endpoint. |
| **Legal Kurdish politics** | Our evidence does not support treating legal parties as PKK-controlled organization. Their public networks and institutional continuity need independent representation. **C0005.** |
| **Government forces** | CT's French/Algerian distinction encodes specific recruitment and vulnerability rules. It should not become a Turkish/Kurdish division of pieces. Guard arrangements require locality and obligations, not presumed ethnic allegiance. **C0003.** |
| **Foreign sanctuary** | CT opens Morocco and Tunisia through a pivotal transition. Syria, KDP and PUK relationships instead require separate, changing permissions and interests. **C0006, C0008.** |
| **Conflict termination** | Neither talks nor Öcalan's capture establishes automatic political victory or organizational destruction. **C0007, C0015.** |

## The largest revision: our initiative system

Our current procedure is **not simply a shortened CT system**.

*Colonial Twilight* determines next initiative from what the **first player** did:

- A multi-location operation or an operation with a Special Activity yields priority.
- A Limited Operation, Event or Pass retains priority.
- Both players passing leaves priority unchanged.

Our draft instead ranks **both players' choices**, awards priority to the smaller action and reverses priority on ties. It also combines broad operations with and without Special Activities.

Those differences can change the entire game. They also expose a wording problem: our operations chapter says passing sacrifices initiative, although our initiative procedure can reward passing with priority.

**Recommendation:** build a CT-derived comparison aid and test it against the current procedure. Do not replace the engine, combat ratios and victory system simultaneously. The test must isolate what initiative changes.
*CT rules 2.3.2–2.3.5; our [operations chapter](research/research-final/package-b-coin-design/08-operations-special-activities.md) and [prototype specification](research/research-final/package-b-coin-design/15-preliminary-design-spec.md).*

## Three worked examples

These positions are constructed rules illustrations, not reconstructed historical operations or reports of actual play. Numerical procedures remain untested design hypotheses.

### 1. Search broadly or preserve initiative

Assume two regions each contain four State troops and one concealed guerrilla. The State has eight resources.

Under our operation costs, securing both regions costs two resources and exposes the guerrillas without removing them. Under a CT-derived initiative rule, that broad operation transfers next-card priority to the PKK.

Securing just one region costs one resource and retains priority, but leaves the other network concealed. The opponent still receives its current response.

This creates a useful choice between coverage and tempo. It does **not** guarantee an uninterrupted search-and-destroy sequence. The test is whether retained priority creates decisions or repetitive lockouts.

### 2. Displacement without erasing responsibility

A rural region has constituency weight one and a protection deficit. A supported displacement event adds an origin record and urban reception pressure in Diyarbakır.

No guerrilla appears automatically. The origin obligation remains. Later relief can reduce current receiving-city strain without erasing the past harm record or automatically improving political confidence.

This deliberately differs from CT's Pop 0 resettlement treatment. The test is whether displacement becomes an efficient way to reduce what a player must protect. If so, our rule fails.
*C0002, C0012; CT 4.2.1.*

### 3. One sanctuary closes while another remains available

KDP access becomes restricted while PUK access remains accommodating. Recruitment in the affected box is suspended, but existing pieces are not automatically deleted.

The PKK can devote effort to movement or accommodation. The State can undertake a cross-border action only with scenario permission and the required cost. Neither player treats the Syrian relationship marker as an ordinary movement space.

This makes sanctuary a relationship problem rather than an on/off bonus. The 1993–1995 scenario must not freely reproduce the diplomatic conditions of 1998.
*C0006–C0008.*

## Two tempting borrowings to reject

**“Commitment becomes Turkish Political Will; France becomes Europe.”**

This looks economical but imports an unsupported relationship between colonial retention, metropolitan deployment, diaspora organization and domestic willingness. European institutions do not collectively function as Ankara's electorate.

**“Guards become Algerian pieces, with Subvert and resettlement copied together.”**

This would combine assumed ethnic allegiance, automatic recruitment and the removal of contested civilians from political accounting. The useful historical relationships require separate treatment.

## Where other COIN games offer stronger guidance

The following mechanical references were checked in the repository's [official rules library](research/rules/md/). They are game-design precedents, not historical evidence about Turkey.

| Reference | Best contribution |
|---|---|
| ***The British Way: Cyprus*** | A smaller initiative menu as a simplicity benchmark. Its next priority follows the chosen boxes, with explicit pass handling. *2.3.2–2.3.5.* |
| ***A Distant Plain*** | Host posture affecting recruitment, exposure and movement. Better guidance for conditional sanctuary than permanently opened countries. Do not copy its automatic posture reset. *6.6.1.* |
| ***People Power*** | Separate government, armed insurgency and nonviolent political agency. The strongest directly checked reference for our legal-political third-player fallback. *1.4, 1.6, 3.3, 7.1.* |
| ***Gandhi*** | Political factions with their own operations rather than armed factions recolored. *1.5, 3.3.* |
| ***Fire in the Lake*** | Shared control with distinct objectives if the state-split hypothesis is reopened. Its allied factions do not themselves justify splitting Turkey's institutions. *1.7–1.8, 7.* |

## Prioritized revision plan

1. **Build the action/response aid.** Compare the current engine with a CT-derived variant; resolve passing, responder scope and Special Activity distinctions.
2. **Preserve independent political and civilian systems.** Simplify markers where possible without merging their meanings.
3. **Close combat and scoring gaps.** Test a limited ambush alternative to our all-or-nothing PKK attack condition; select one explicit checkpoint and final-scoring procedure.
4. **Specify host decisions and deck boundaries.** Define diplomacy's actual effects and keep later crisis assumptions out of the early scenario.
5. **Inspect Peru's final rules before expanding the reference claim.** Its publisher-described mechanisms warrant serious comparison, but not unverified transplantation.

## Final judgment

*Colonial Twilight* should be our **primary working mechanical reference**. It should not be our sole historical model or be declared the strongest of every published COIN game. Its procedures provide a strong experiment; only recorded testing can establish whether adapting them improves our design.
